Getting the inputs right

  • Use a payslip from a plain month: no bonus, no share vest, no arrears. The tax deducted in such a month is what repeats.
  • Shares bought through salary and RSUs are a separate investment. They are kept out here so nothing on this page depends on your employer's share price.
  • Vest months usually take extra tax out of cash pay. That is ignored here because it belongs to the share side of the ledger.

What this page assumes

  • Yearly expenses are spread over 12 months. In reality trips and festivals land in particular months, so keep a buffer.
  • Tax on the bonus is at the rate you set. For most people that is the marginal slab plus surcharge and cess.
  • The yearly emergencies line is spending, not the emergency fund. Keep six months of total outgo in a liquid fund before any of this is deployed.

What the numbers don't capture

Index SIP

  • Returns are not smooth. A 10-year Nifty 50 SIP has historically landed anywhere between roughly 8% and 16% a year depending on the start date. Use the slider to see how much of the lead survives at 10%.
  • Fully liquid. Money is back in your account in two working days, which matters if a job change or medical need comes up.
  • No effort after the first setup. Step-up the SIP as income rises.

Gold ETF

  • Gold is insurance, not a growth engine. It has had decade-long flat stretches. It shines when equity and the rupee both fall.
  • A sensible role is 5 to 15% of the SIP, not the whole ₹30K.
  • Gains are taxed at 12.5% after a year with no ₹1.25L exemption, unlike equity.

Rental property (PG)

  • Leverage cuts both ways. At 6% appreciation the loan magnifies gains; at 2% the EMI drains you for years with little to show.
  • A PG is a small business: tenant churn, repairs, a caretaker, and local rules on paying guests. Budget your time, not just money.
  • Illiquid and lumpy. Selling takes months and costs about 2% plus capital-gains tax. You cannot sell one room.
  • An empty plot earns nothing and may cost you a wall and security. It is purely a bet on appreciation, with a pricier loan.

Prepay the home loan

  • A guaranteed, tax-free return equal to your loan rate. No market return is guaranteed, so compare it with equity only after a haircut for risk.
  • Under the old tax regime, interest up to ₹2L a year is deductible, which lowers the true cost of the loan by about a third at the 30% slab. That makes prepaying less attractive than it looks.
  • Money paid into the loan cannot be taken back. Keep a 6-month emergency fund before prepaying anything.
  • Peace of mind has a value the chart cannot show. Many people split: prepay part, SIP the rest.
